Availability, SLIs, SLOs & SLAs

  • SLI = measure: what actually happened (e.g. % of requests that succeeded).
  • SLO = target: what the team aims for (e.g. 99.9% over 30 days).
  • SLA = promise: what customers are contractually owed if you miss it.
  • Error budget = 100% − SLO: the failure you can afford to spend on shipping changes.

Why SLO is stricter than SLA

The internal target (99.9%) trips long before the contract (99.5%) does. When the error budget runs out, the team slows risky releases before customers are owed service credits.

Added

Series multiplies: a request needing three 99.9% components succeeds 99.9% × 99.9% × 99.9% ≈ 99.7% of the time, worse than any single part. Redundancy adds nines: two independent 99% copies give 1 − (0.01)² = 99.99%, provided failover works and the copies don't fail together.

Added

Availability = MTBF ÷ (MTBF + MTTR). MTBF is the mean time between failures, MTTR the mean time to recover. Halving recovery time helps as much as halving failures, which is why fast rollbacks and alerts matter.

Added

Two ways to measure: time-based ("up 99.9% of minutes") or request-based ("99.9% of requests succeeded"). Request-based SLIs reflect what users actually felt, including partial outages.

Check yourself

Answer out loud before you open each card.

"99.95% of requests succeeded last month." SLI, SLO or SLA?Show answer

SLI: a measurement of what happened. The SLO is the target it's compared against.

What is the monthly error budget for a 99.9% SLO?Show answer

0.1% ≈ 43.2 minutes of full downtime in a 30-day month, or the request-weighted equivalent.

Why not aim for 100%?Show answer

Each nine costs far more and users can't tell 99.999% from 100% through their own Wi-Fi. A budget lets teams trade reliability for speed deliberately.

Your API is 99.99% but depends on a 99.9% database. Best case for the API?Show answer

About 99.89%. Series availability multiplies, so the API can't beat its weakest required dependency.

The site returns pages fast, but with the wrong prices. Available? Reliable?Show answer

Available, not reliable. Availability is about being usable; reliability also requires behaving correctly.

Budget is exhausted on day 12. What changes?Show answer

Freeze risky releases and spend effort on reliability (tests, rollbacks, capacity) until the budget recovers.
