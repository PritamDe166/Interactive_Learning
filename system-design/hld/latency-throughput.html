Latency, Percentiles & Queueing

  • Latency: how long one request takes.
  • Throughput: how many requests finish per second.
  • Percentiles (p50 / p95 / p99) show how latency is spread, especially the slow tail.
  • Near capacity, requests queue, and latency rises much faster than load.

Four numbers, four questions

Latency · how long?

Time for one request, end to end: network + queueing + processing + dependencies. Example: 120 ms.

Throughput · how much?

Work finished per unit time. Example: 10,000 RPS. High throughput can coexist with high latency.

Concurrency · how many at once?

Requests in flight right now. Linked to the other two by Little's Law: concurrency = throughput × latency.

What to notice

Added

Why the curve bends: for a simple queue, average time in system ≈ service time ÷ (1 − utilisation). At 50% load that's 2× the service time, at 90% it's 10×, and at 99% it's 100×. Real systems aim to run their bottleneck well below 100%.

Added

Overload feeds itself. Timeouts trigger client retries, retries add load, and more load means more timeouts. This is a retry storm, and Week 15 (backoff, circuit breakers) exists to stop it.

Why can p99 matter more than p50 for a page that calls 50 backends?Show answer

Fan-out amplifies the tail: 1 − 0.99⁵⁰ ≈ 40% of page loads wait on at least one p99-slow call.

A batch job or a deep queue: millions of items per hour, but each waits minutes. A truck full of hard drives has huge throughput too.
