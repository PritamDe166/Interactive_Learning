Class Relationships

  • There are five relationships, from loosest to tightest. Dependency: uses another object briefly. Association: keeps a reference to it. Aggregation: has parts that keep living after the whole is gone. Composition: owns parts that are deleted with the whole. Inheritance: is a kind of another class.
  • Decide by lifetime and ownership: who creates the part, who else holds it, does it outlive the whole?
  • In .NET, "dies with the whole" means nothing else references it, so the garbage collector reclaims it.
  • is-a → inheritance; has-a → composition. Favour composition: combine parts instead of multiplying subclasses.

3Revise in two minutes

Cheat-sheet

  • Only a parameter, local or static call→dependency
  • Kept in a field or property, nobody owns anybody→association (injected services too)
  • Parts created elsewhere, passed in, can be shared→aggregation (department ↔ doctors)
  • Whole creates its parts and keeps them to itself→composition (order → order lines)
  • "B is a kind of A" is true, and B can be used anywhere an A is expected without surprises→inheritance
  • You'd override something to throw NotImplementedException→not really is-a: use composition
  • Subclasses multiply (channel × style)→composition over inheritance: hold the parts
  • Composite parts hold files or connections→the whole implements IDisposable and disposes them

Inheritance makes one object; composition makes several. A Patient : Person is a single heap object containing Person's fields plus Patient's. A Car with an Engine is two objects and a reference, so the engine can be swapped or faked.

GC timing isn't deterministic. Unreachable objects are reclaimed at the collector's next run, not at the moment the whole "dies". Use IDisposable / using for anything that must be released at a known time.

Aggregation is loosely defined in UML; many designers draw a plain association instead. Composition has a clear meaning: exclusive ownership and a shared lifetime.

4Test yourself

Which relationship is it?

Explain it out loud

Answer before you open each card.

Aggregation vs composition: one question that decides it?Show answer

"If I delete the whole, do the parts go too, and can a part belong to two wholes?" Parts go and are exclusive → composition. Parts survive or are shared → aggregation.

Dependency vs association?Show answer

A dependency is temporary: a parameter, local variable or static call. An association is structural: a reference kept in a field or property.

You set asha = null. Is the Patient object collected?Show answer

No, as long as an Appointment still references it: an association keeps the patient reachable. Try it in the lab.

Why shouldn't Stack inherit from List?Show answer

It would expose Insert, RemoveAt and the indexer, letting callers break last-in-first-out. A stack has a list and exposes only Push, Pop and Peek.

How does composition usually look in the database?Show answer

A required foreign key with ON DELETE CASCADE (order → order lines). Aggregation is a nullable foreign key or a link table.

Give two advantages of composition over inheritance.Show answer

Parts can be swapped at runtime and faked in tests, and variations add classes instead of multiplying them. A base-class change doesn't ripple into every subclass.
