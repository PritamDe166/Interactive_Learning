Read Replicas, Multi-AZ & Multi-Region

  • Multi-AZ: a synchronous standby in another AZ for high availability and automatic failover.
  • Read replicas: asynchronous, read-only copies to scale reads; you promote one by hand.
  • Cross-Region replicas: faster reads far away and disaster recovery.
  • They solve different problems and are often combined.

In terms you know

AWSClosest thing you knowThe difference
Multi-AZA SQL Server Always On availability group with a synchronous secondary and automatic failoverOne checkbox; the secondary isn't readable in the classic form.
Read replicaA readable asynchronous secondary (or log-shipped reporting copy)Has its own endpoint; you send reports to it.

Points to remember

  1. Multi-AZ: standby in another AZ, synchronous replication, automatic failover to the same endpoint. For HA, not for reads (classic form).
  2. Read replica: asynchronous, read-only, own endpoint; same or another Region; can be promoted manually.
  3. Cross-Region replica: DR plus lower latency reads for distant users; cross-Region transfer is billed.
  4. Writes always go to the primary.
  5. Multi-AZ DB cluster (newer): two readable standbys across 3 AZs.
  6. Aurora supports up to 15 replicas sharing one storage volume.

Cheat-sheet

  • High availability, automatic failover→Multi-AZ
  • Read-heavy workload, offload reports→Read replica
  • DR or low-latency reads in another Region→Cross-Region read replica
  • Synchronous replication→Multi-AZ
  • Asynchronous replication→Read replica
  • Turn a replica into its own database→Promote it

Read replica limits depend on the engine; Aurora allows up to 15. Check the engine's page before quoting a number for RDS.

Snapshot copy to another Region is the cheaper DR option when you can accept a longer recovery than a cross-Region replica.

Not in a Multi-AZ instance deployment. (A Multi-AZ DB cluster has readable standbys.)

No, asynchronous.

Promote it to a standalone primary.

No, Multi-AZ failover keeps the same DNS endpoint.
