IaaS, PaaS, SaaS & pricing

  • Every app runs on a stack of layers, from networking up to your data. The models differ in who manages which layers.
  • IaaS (Amazon EC2): AWS runs the hardware and virtualisation; you manage the OS and up.
  • PaaS (AWS Elastic Beanstalk): you bring code and data; the platform runs everything else.
  • SaaS (Gmail, Zoom): you just use the finished app, but your users, settings and data are still yours.
  • Cloud pricing charges for compute, storage and data transfer out; data coming in is generally free.

In everyday terms: pizza as a service

A widely used way to picture the four models. The more you buy as a service, the less you do yourself.

Points to remember

  1. Nine layers: networking, storage, servers, virtualisation, OS, middleware, runtime, application, data. The models differ in who manages which.
  2. IaaS: provider runs hardware and virtualisation; you manage OS and up. AWS example: EC2. Most control; easiest lift-and-shift.
  3. PaaS: provider also runs OS, middleware and runtime; you bring code and data. AWS example: Elastic Beanstalk.
  4. SaaS: a finished app (Gmail, Zoom, Dropbox). You still manage users, access, settings and the data you put in.
  5. Most AWS services are managed building blocks, not SaaS.
  6. Pricing: you pay for compute (time/capacity), storage (GB-month + requests) and data transfer out. Data in is generally free.
  7. Savings aren't automatic: idle resources and data transfer out still cost money.

    Exam cheat-sheet

    • Rent virtual servers, choose and patch the OS→IaaS · Amazon EC2
    • Upload code, no servers or patching→PaaS · AWS Elastic Beanstalk
    • Finished product, users just log in→SaaS
    • Most control / easiest lift-and-shift from on-prem→IaaS
    • Data going into AWS→Generally free
    • Data going out to the internet→Charged per GB

    Corrected

    In SaaS, data is not purely the provider's job. The notes' layer diagram colours SaaS "Data" as provider-managed, while their own table says shared. The table is right: the provider runs the app, but you decide who has access, what you store and how it's configured.

    Added

    Not every managed AWS service is SaaS. Most AWS services (RDS, Lambda, S3) are managed building blocks you use to build your own app. SaaS means a complete end-user application. Amazon Connect, a ready-to-use contact centre, is an AWS example closer to SaaS.

    Added

    Savings are not automatic. Idle resources you forget to switch off, data transfer out and the wrong pricing option can make the cloud expensive. Pay-as-you-go rewards switching things off.

    Check yourself

    Answer out loud before you open each card.

    Who patches the operating system on an EC2 instance?Show answer

    You. EC2 is IaaS: AWS manages up to the virtualisation layer; the OS and everything above is yours.

    A team uploads a .NET app and never touches servers. Which model and which AWS service?Show answer

    PaaS, AWS Elastic Beanstalk.

    Which model gives you the most control, and why is that also its downside?Show answer

    IaaS (in the cloud). More control means more to manage: OS patches, runtimes, middleware.

    With SaaS, is everything the provider's responsibility?Show answer

    No. You still manage users, access, settings and what data you put in.

    Name the three things cloud pricing charges for.Show answer

    Compute, storage and data transfer (out).

    Uploading 1 TB of backups into AWS: does the transfer itself cost money?Show answer

    Generally no: data in is free. You pay to store it, and you'd pay to transfer it back out.
