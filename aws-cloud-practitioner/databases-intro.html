Databases on AWS

  • A database adds structure, indexes and queries on top of storage.
  • Managed databases (RDS, DynamoDB…) hand patching, backups and failover to AWS; on EC2 you do it all.
  • Relational: tables, SQL, JOINs. NoSQL: flexible items (often JSON), built to scale out.
  • AWS offers a purpose-built database for each data shape.

Points to remember

  1. Relational = tables, rows, columns, SQL, JOINs, fixed schema. AWS: RDS, Aurora.
  2. NoSQL = non-relational, flexible schema, often JSON; types: key-value, document, graph, in-memory, search.
  3. Relational traditionally scales vertically; NoSQL scales horizontally.
  4. Managed database: AWS handles provisioning, OS and engine patching, backups, monitoring, HA. You cannot SSH into it.
  5. Self-managed on EC2: you own patching, backups, HA and scaling.
  6. You always own your data, schema, users and network access.

    Cheat-sheet

    • Least operational overhead for a database→Managed service (RDS, DynamoDB…)
    • SQL, JOINs, fixed schema→Relational
    • Flexible schema, JSON documents→NoSQL
    • Full OS control of the database server→Database on EC2
    • Who patches RDS?→AWS
    • Who manages the schema and data?→The customer

    Reference

    TypeShape of dataAWS service
    RelationalTables and relationsRDS, Aurora
    Key-value / documentItems or JSON documentsDynamoDB, DocumentDB
    GraphNodes and relationshipsNeptune
    In-memoryCache, sessionsElastiCache
    Time seriesReadings over timeTimestream
    LedgerShared, tamper-evident recordsManaged Blockchain
    Data warehouseColumns for analyticsRedshift

    "Relational never scales out" is too strong. Read replicas and Aurora spread reads, and Aurora storage grows automatically. The exam only expects the general contrast.
